A. Core Language & Browser Pipeline
Simulation Kernel (Rust →→ WebAssembly): All astrodynamics, thermodynamics, fluid networks, structural stresses, and economic accounting run in pure Rust compiled to WASM.
Rendering Engine (Rust + WebGL2 / Canvas2D Hybrid): Avoid heavy game engines like Unreal or Unity, and even avoid heavy PBR pipelines in Three.js. Instead, use lightweight direct WebGL2 bindings in Rust (via glow or wgpu configured for WebGL2 compatibility on integrated chips) paired with ultra-crisp 2D vector rendering for instruments, CAD blueprints, and telemetry screens.
Why no 3D models works in your favor: Real rockets, engines, habitats, and planets are mathematical surfaces of revolution and parametric extrusions.
A rocket nozzle is a mathematical Rao parabolic bell curve defined by throat radius (RtRt​), expansion ratio (ϵϵ), and length.
A propellant tank is a cylinder capped by ellipsoidal or hemispherical domes with a specific aspect ratio.
An O'Neill cylinder, a Whipple shield, a solar array truss, or a reaction wheel are all pure geometric primitives.
When the player changes chamber pressure or tank volume, your Rust code computes the exact vertex coordinates via parametric equations in microseconds and uploads a tiny, ultra-clean mesh to the browser. Total download size for every possible spacecraft part in the universe: 0 KB of 3D assets.
B. How to Achieve "Near-Zero CPU Usage"
Games like Kerbal Space Program destroy CPUs because they calculate physics on every part of a spacecraft 50 times per second, even when nothing is happening. Odderon will use Event-Driven Analytical Propagation:
Zero-Cost Analytical Orbits (On-Rails): The 50,000 asteroids, planets, moons, and coasting spacecraft are never updated frame-by-frame in a loop. Their positions are stored as Keplerian Elements + J2J2​ secular drift equations (or Chebyshev polynomial ephemeris tables). To find where Mars or a player's satellite is at time tt, the CPU only evaluates the equation for the objects currently visible on your screen or sensor display. Whether 10 ships or 1,000,000 ships are orbiting across the galaxy, idle CPU usage is 0.0%.
High-Precision Numerical Integration Only When Thrusting or in Atmosphere: Only the spacecraft you are actively flying (or running a burn prediction for) uses a high-order numerical integrator (such as a Symplectic Integrator or RKF45 / IAS15). The moment the engine cuts off in vacuum and torques stabilize, the state vector (r⃗,v⃗)(r,v) converts back into an analytical orbit, and the CPU goes back to sleep.
Render-On-Demand for Engineering & Company Views: When you are at the CAD terminal, the Telemetry Console, or the CEO spreadsheet, the screen only redraws when a value changes or you move the camera. Your laptop fan won't even spin up.
C. Scaling from 4,000 to 8,000+ Light-Years (Solving the Floating-Point "Kraken")
Standard 64-bit floats (f64) have ~15–17 decimal digits of precision. One light-year is ∼9.46×1015 meters∼9.46×1015 meters. At 8,000 light-years (∼7.5×1019 meters∼7.5×1019 meters), standard floats lose millimeter precision and your spacecraft will jitter apart.
The Solution: Hierarchical Reference Frames + i128 Fixed-Point Math.
Never store a spacecraft's position relative to Earth when it is in another star system. Store coordinates in a 3-tier hierarchy:
Galactic Sector Coordinate (i128 integers): Measures position in Parsecs/Light-Years from the Solar System Barycenter. Because i128 has 38 decimal digits of precision, it can span the entire observable universe down to the nanometer without losing a single bit of accuracy!
Local Barycentric Frame (f64): Position relative to the local star system or planet's center of mass (accurate to micrometers across a solar system).
Vessel Local Frame (f64): The cockpit and structural physics always keep (0,0,0)(0,0,0) at the spacecraft’s exact Center of Mass. You can scale Odderon to 100,000 light-years (the entire Milky Way) tomorrow and zero code will break.
